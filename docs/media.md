# Images and videos

Collectors save media locations in revision metadata, not media bytes. Web articles, feed enclosures and Telegram albums supply images and native video attachments. Embedded players that expose only a player page, such as a YouTube iframe, are not downloadable video attachments.

When preparing a story, the bot downloads media with bounded sizes and deduplicates across its current reports and relevant research pages. Exact size and SHA-256 matches keep the first copy. Videos with similar duration are compared using frames one third into each video. Different durations skip visual comparison. Grayscale thumbnail differences reject obvious nonmatches. Ambiguous comparisons send two compressed WebP images to the vision model for structured visual evidence; the text-only JEV endpoint makes the final identity decision from that evidence. Failed or unreadable comparisons keep both copies.

Summarization and review receive text plus deduplicated still images. Model-only WebP copies use quality 90 and fit inside 2560×2560 without upscaling, preserving detail for readable text. The encoder emits complete image packets and validates RIFF/chunk lengths before model dispatch; the FFmpeg 5 WebP file muxer cannot safely rewrite large containers on stdout. Telegram receives original bytes without bot-side recompression. New posts prefer a compatible photo/video album; if no native photo or video is available, they use a document-only album. Incompatible formats are not split into extra messages. Videos and video frames are excluded. `MEDIA_SUMMARY_MAX_IMAGES` defaults to 10 and accepts 1 through 10. `MEDIA_VIDEO_DURATION_TOLERANCE` defaults to 0.2, a fraction of the longer duration. The minimum video-duration tolerance is two seconds.

Downloaded media is temporary. `tmp/media-cache/` contains per-story files, removed after confirmed attachment delivery. Files expire after seven days, checked at startup and hourly. Video decoding uses memory where possible and deletes seekable fallback files immediately; abandoned decoder files expire after 24 hours. Keep `tmp/` private. No media cache is mounted as a permanent Docker volume. Source media older than seven days after collection is not downloaded again.

New media posts are one album of up to ten deduplicated attachments with the news text as the first item's caption. A single attachment uses its native send method with the same caption. Telegram's [album limit](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api#sendmediagroup) is 2 to 10 items, and the [caption limit](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api#inputmediaphoto) is 1024 characters after entity parsing. Captions retain the headline, news text, up to three compact primary-first source links and bounded review/context notes. The renderer shortens the caption without cutting HTML entities, URLs or UTF-16 surrogate pairs. Stored snapshots retain the full text and source inventory. Later text updates edit the caption. Text-only posts keep the 4096-character limit. Excess attachments are not sent as follow-up messages. PostgreSQL temporarily spools upload bytes before dispatch for restart safety. Confirmed uploads erase those bytes immediately. Startup and hourly maintenance erase unpublished or unresolved bytes after seven days. Telegram message IDs and hashes remain for deletion and audit; media bytes do not become a permanent archive. Database backups/WAL can retain prior temporary data according to the operator's database-retention policy.

Explicit temporary Telegram rejections can retry. Unknown upload outcomes require review instead of blind resend. An older text-first post with unfinished attachments has `publication_media_*` diagnostics and does not count as a fully successful run-now publication. Deleting the post also deletes its known attachment messages. Already-sent text-first publications retain their legacy edit/recovery handling and are not resent or converted automatically.

The runtime requires `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe`; the Docker image installs both. Web media fetches use the same public-address validation, DNS pinning, redirect checks and streaming byte limits as research fetches. Limits are 9 MB per image, 45 MB per video and 256 MB and 100 unique download locations per story. Unsupported or unavailable downloads stop preparation rather than silently publishing without requested media.
